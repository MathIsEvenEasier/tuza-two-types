\begin{abstract}
Tuza's conjecture states that the minimum number of edges meeting every
triangle of a graph is at most twice the maximum number of edge-disjoint
triangles. We prove it for every finite simple split graph whose
independent-side vertices of degree at least two have at most two distinct
neighborhoods. The clique size, the neighborhood overlap, and both
multiplicities are unrestricted. The proof first truncates multiplicities
without changing either optimum. Matching decompositions and sums of
vertex labels give triangle packings; cuts give triangle covers.
We compare these bounds by exact finite checks for clique sizes at most
42 and a rational interval certificate for all larger sizes. The graph
reductions, certificate soundness, domain coverage, and final theorem
are formalized in Lean.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and proof outline}
For a finite simple graph $G$, a \emph{triangle packing} is a family of
pairwise edge-disjoint triangles. A \emph{triangle cover} is a set of
edges meeting every triangle. Write $\tp(G)$ for the largest packing
size and $\tc(G)$ for the smallest cover size. Tuza's conjecture, posed
in 1981, asserts that $\tc(G)\leq2\tp(G)$ for every $G$.

A \emph{split partition} is a partition $V(G)=C\mathbin{\dot\cup}I$
in which $C$ is a clique and $I$ is independent. A vertex of $I$ is
\emph{active} if it has at least two neighbors in $C$; otherwise it
belongs to no triangle. Its \emph{type} is its neighborhood in $C$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:two-types-all}
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph with a split partition
$V(G)=C\mathbin{\dot\cup}I$. If the active vertices of $I$ have at most
two distinct neighborhoods in $C$, then
\[
 \tc(G)\leq2\tp(G).
\]
\end{theorem}

The theorem imposes no bound on $|C|$ or on the number of vertices of
either type. It proves a case of Tuza's conjecture; the general
conjecture and the case of arbitrary split graphs remain open.
Bonamy et al.~\cite{bonamy} proved the conjecture for threshold graphs,
which include split graphs with nested outside neighborhoods.
Chahua and Guti\'errez~\cite{dense} proved it for split graphs of order
$n$ and minimum degree at least $3n/5$.
Zeng~\cite{zeng} proved the two-type case for a specified clique part
of size eight, allowing arbitrary multiplicities. Theorem~\ref{thm:two-types-all}
removes that bound on the clique size.
Huang et al.~\cite{epi} subsequently reported the case of three active
neighborhood types with a specified eight-vertex clique part. The two
results relax different restrictions: here the number of types is at
most two, while the clique size is arbitrary.

The argument has two computational parts with explicit graph-theoretic
inputs. Section~\ref{sec:compression} bounds the multiplicities while
preserving both optima. Sections~\ref{sec:packing} and~\ref{sec:covers}
construct packings and covers. Section~\ref{sec:finite} compares their
sizes for $2\leq |C|\leq42$. For larger cliques,
Section~\ref{sec:analytic} proves several parameter ranges directly;
Section~\ref{sec:interval} covers the remaining range by one certificate
on a bounded real domain. That certificate handles every $|C|\geq43$
at once. Section~\ref{sec:formal} describes the Lean theorem and its
public source build.

\paragraph{Notation.}
After removing inactive vertices, write $S,T\subseteq C$ for the
two neighborhoods and $p,q$ for their multiplicities. Put
\[
 k=|C|,\quad s=|S|,\quad t=|T|,\quad u=|S\cap T|,
\]
and partition the core into
\[
 A=S\setminus T,\quad B=T\setminus S,\quad U=S\cap T,
 \quad O=C\setminus(S\cup T).
\]
Their sizes are $s-u,t-u,u,k-s-t+u$. Thus, after ordering $s\leq t$,
\[
 2\leq s\leq t\leq k,\qquad \max(0,s+t-k)\leq u\leq s.
\]
A single type is represented by $T=S$ and $q=0$; the clique alone by
$p=q=0$. For $k\geq2$ these conventions allow a dummy set $S$ of size
at least two. Write $z_+=\max(0,z)$, and set
\[
 c_k=\binom{k}{2}-\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor,
 \qquad
 g(k)=\begin{cases}0,&k\leq2,\\
 \lceil(k-1)(k-2)/6\rceil,&k\geq3.
 \end{cases}
\]
Both are bounds used below: $c_k$ is the number of core edges deleted
by a balanced cut, and $g(k)$ is a guaranteed clique packing size.

\section{Compressing equal neighborhoods}\label{sec:compression}

Let $H$ be any finite
simple graph and let $S\subseteq V(H)$ have size $s\geq2$. Form $G_m$ by
adding $m$ independent vertices, each with neighborhood exactly $S$.
The set $S$ need not be a clique.
Define
\[
 h(s)=\chi'(K_s)=
 \begin{cases}s-1,&s\text{ even},\\s,&s\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
For completeness, the required round-robin decomposition is explicit.
If $s$ is even, label the vertices by $\{\infty\}\cup\mathbb Z/(s-1)\mathbb Z$.
For each residue $c$, take the matching
\[
 \{\{\infty,c\}\}\ \cup\
 \{\{c+i,c-i\}:1\leq i\leq(s-2)/2\}.
\]
It is perfect: the nonzero residues modulo the odd number $s-1$ occur
exactly once in pairs $\{i,-i\}$. Every edge not incident with $\infty$
belongs to exactly one matching, because its endpoints $a,b$ determine
$c=(a+b)/2$ modulo $s-1$; division by $2$ is valid for an odd modulus.
Edges incident with $\infty$ have their evident unique color. This also
covers $s=2$, when the second set is empty. For odd $s$, use this
construction on $s+1$ vertices and delete one vertex. The resulting
$s$ matchings each have $(s-1)/2$ edges. Thus every color class has
$\lfloor s/2\rfloor$ edges. The degree lower bound for even $s$, and
edge counting with at most $\lfloor s/2\rfloor$ edges per color for odd
$s$, also prove that the displayed number of colors is minimal.




\begin{theorem}[Exact multiplicity compression]\label{thm:compression}
For every $m\geq0$,
\[
 \tp(G_m)=\tp(G_{\min(m,h(s))}),\qquad
 \tc(G_m)=\tc(G_{\min(m,s-1)}).
\]
Consequently truncating at $h(s)$ preserves both parameters simultaneously.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both parameters are nondecreasing under addition of vertices, so it suffices
to prove the reverse inequalities above their proposed thresholds.

Take any triangle packing in $G_m$. Every triangle using a new vertex uses
one edge of $H[S]$. Collect these edges into a graph $J\subseteq H[S]$.
They are all distinct. Restrict a fixed $h(s)$-edge-coloring of $K_S$ to $J$.
Replace the center of each such triangle by the new vertex indexed by the
color of its clique edge. Triangles assigned to the same center use a matching,
so their spokes are distinct. Triangles assigned to different centers have
different spokes, and their clique edges are distinct. All triangles lying
inside $H$ are left unchanged; they use none of these clique edges and no
new spokes. Thus the packing survives on $h(s)$ new vertices with the same
cardinality.

For covers, let $r=s-1$ and take any triangle cover $F$ of $G_r$.
Put $F_H=F\cap E(H)$ and let $J$ consist of the edges of $H[S]$ not in $F_H$.
For every new vertex $x$, the set $Z_x=\{v\in S:xv\in F\}$ is a vertex cover
of $J$: the triangle formed by $x$ and an edge of $J$ must be met by a
selected spoke. Choose $x_*$ minimizing $|Z_x|$. Every edge of $J$ is
incident to $Z_{x_*}$, and each vertex of $S$ has at most $s-1$ neighbors
in $S$. Consequently
\[
 |E(J)|\leq (s-1)|Z_{x_*}|
 \leq \sum_{x=1}^{r}|Z_x|.
\]
The sum counts distinct selected spokes. Replace them all by $E(J)$.
The resulting set $F_H\cup E(J)$ has size at most $|F|$, still covers every
triangle in $H$, and contains every edge of $H[S]$. It therefore covers the
triangles centered at arbitrarily many new vertices. This directly
transforms every cover, without introducing a separate vertex-cover optimum.
\end{proof}



Apply the theorem to one type at a time, taking all other vertices as
the old graph $H$. We may henceforth assume
\[
 0\leq p\leq h(s),\qquad 0\leq q\leq h(t).
\]
The two types need not be disjoint or incomparable for this reduction.

\section{Packing bounds}\label{sec:packing}
A triangle meeting $I$ has one outside vertex, called its center, and
one core edge. Assigning a matching of core edges to a center produces
edge-disjoint triangles: the matching condition prevents reuse of a
spoke. Assigning disjoint sets of core edges to different centers
prevents reuse of a core edge. In particular, $r\leq h(d)$ centers
with a common $d$-vertex neighborhood support $r\lfloor d/2\rfloor$
triangles, by assigning $r$ matching classes of the coloring above.

\subsection{Two ways to select matching classes}
Put $e_S=\binom{s}{2}$, $e_T=\binom{t}{2}$ and $e_U=\binom{u}{2}$.
Independently choose $p$ of the $h(s)$ matching classes on $S$ and
$q$ of the $h(t)$ matching classes on $T$, uniformly among such subsets.
An edge in both selections is kept only for the first type. Deleting
edges preserves the matching property. Each edge in $S$, respectively
$T$, is selected with probability $r_S=p/h(s)$, respectively
$r_T=q/h(t)$. The expected number of distinct selected edges is
$r_Se_S+r_Te_T-r_Sr_Te_U$. Some selection therefore gives an
outside-centered packing of size at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:allocation-bound}
 L=\left\lceil r_Se_S+r_Te_T-r_Sr_Te_U\right\rceil.
\end{equation}

The second construction coordinates the colors on the intersection.

\begin{theorem}[Two local colorings with controlled overlap]\label{thm:coupled}
Let $s,t\geq2$, $d=\max(s,t)$, and $H=h(d)$. Put
\[
 \bar p=\min(p,H),\qquad \bar q=\min(q,H),\qquad
 z=\max(0,\bar p+\bar q-H).
\]
There is an outside-centered packing of size at least
\[
 \boxed{L_{\mathrm c}=\left\lceil
 \frac{\bar p\binom s2+\bar q\binom t2-z\binom u2}{H}
 \right\rceil.}
\]
If $s=t$ and $p+q\leq h(s)$, the maximum size of an outside-centered
packing is exactly
\[
 \boxed{(p+q)\lfloor s/2\rfloor.}
\]
These statements allow arbitrary overlap and arbitrary $o$.
The last identity concerns only outside-centered triangles, not the
maximum packing in the whole graph.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an $H$-edge-coloring of $K_d$. Embed each of $S,T$ injectively in its
vertex set, using the same labels for vertices of $S\cap T$. This is
possible because the labels used by $S\setminus T$ and $T\setminus S$
may overlap: these are two separate embeddings, not an embedding of their
union. Pull back the coloring to each clique. Each local color class is
a matching, and a shared edge receives the same color in both cliques.
There is no requirement that the coloring be proper on $K_{S\cup T}$.

Identify the colors with $\mathbb Z/H\mathbb Z$. Fix palettes $A,B$
of sizes $\bar p,\bar q$ with $|A\cap B|=z$: choose $B$ from the
complement of $A$ until it is exhausted, then use the necessary colors
of $A$. For each $r\in\mathbb Z/H\mathbb Z$, use the palettes
\[
 A_r=\{c:c+r\in A\},\qquad B_r=\{c:c+r\in B\}.
\]
They retain the prescribed sizes and intersection. Assign their colors
to distinct outside centers within each type. Retain any edge selected
in both types only for the first type. All remaining triangles are
edge-disjoint: within a center their core edges form a matching,
between centers the spokes are distinct, and no core edge is retained
twice.

Let $J_r,K_r$ be the selected core-edge sets. As $r$ runs through all
$H$ shifts, each edge of $S$ is selected exactly $\bar p$ times and
each edge of $T$ exactly $\bar q$ times. Each common edge is selected
by both exactly $z$ times, because its color agrees in the two
neighborhoods. Summing the union identity gives
\[
 \sum_r |J_r\cup K_r|+z\binom u2
   =\bar p\binom s2+\bar q\binom t2.
\]
A shift maximizing the union has size at least its finite average.
Taking a ceiling proves the bound. This is a deterministic finite-sum
argument; no probability formalization is needed.

If $s=t$, every color class in the round-robin factorization of $K_s$ has
size $\lfloor s/2\rfloor$. When $p+q\leq H$, assign disjoint color sets to
the two types. No shared edge is selected twice, so every outside center
attains this many triangles. No outside center can support more: its
triangles must use disjoint pairs of its $s$ spokes. This proves exactness.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Greedy completion in the core]\label{lem:completion}
Let $G$ be any split graph with a $k$-vertex clique and an outside-centered
packing of size $\ell$. Define
\[
 c_k=\binom k2-\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor.
\]
Then this particular packing can be extended to a packing of size at least
\[
 \boxed{\max\left\{\ell,
      \left\lceil\frac{c_k+2\ell}{3}\right\rceil\right\}.}
\]
There is no restriction on neighborhood types.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Delete the $\ell$ core edges used by the given packing. Repeatedly choose
a triangle in the remaining core and delete its three edges, until there
is no triangle. If $b$ triangles were chosen, the leftover graph has
$\binom k2-\ell-3b$ edges. Mantel's bound gives
\[
 \binom k2-\ell-3b\leq\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor,
 \qquad
 \ell+b\geq\left\lceil\frac{c_k+2\ell}{3}\right\rceil.
\]
The new triangles use neither selected core edges nor any spokes, so the
two packings combine. The lower bound $\ell$ is automatic.

For completeness, Mantel's bound here has a short proof. In a
triangle-free graph with $k>0$ vertices and $e>0$ edges, every edge $vw$
satisfies $d(v)+d(w)\leq k$. Summing over edges and applying
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 4e^2/k\leq\sum_v d(v)^2
   =\sum_{vw\in E}(d(v)+d(w))\leq ke.
\]
Hence $e\leq k^2/4$; the zero cases and the integer rounding are immediate.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Packing triangles by sums of labels}

\begin{lemma}[Triangle packing by sums of labels]\label{lem:sum-coloring}
Let $H$ be a finite simple graph on $n\geq1$ vertices, with $t(H)$
triangles. Its triangles can be assigned $n$ colors so that triangles
of the same color are edge-disjoint. Consequently,
\[
 \tp(H)\geq\left\lceil\frac{t(H)}n\right\rceil.
\]
In particular, applying this to $K_n$ gives the same guaranteed value
$g(n)=\lceil(n-1)(n-2)/6\rceil$ for $n\geq3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Label the vertices bijectively by $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$. Color each
triangle by the sum of its three labels. If two triangles share an edge
with labels $a,b$ and have the same color, their third labels $c,d$
satisfy $a+b+c=a+b+d$, hence $c=d$. The triangles are therefore equal.
Each color class is an edge-disjoint packing. All $t(H)$ triangles lie
in these $n$ classes, so the largest class has at least $t(H)/n$
triangles; its size is an integer. For $K_n$, use
$t(K_n)=\binom n3$ and $\binom n3/n=(n-1)(n-2)/6$.
\end{proof}



For later use define
\[
 W(r,z)=\left\lceil
 \frac{\binom{k}{3}+r\binom{s}{2}+z\binom{t}{2}}{k+r+z}
 \right\rceil.
\]
This counts all triangles in the induced graph consisting of the core,
$r$ centers of type $S$, and $z$ centers of type $T$, then applies
Lemma~\ref{lem:sum-coloring}. In particular, $W(p,q)$, $W(p,0)$ and
$W(0,q)$ are lower bounds on $\tp(G)$.
Combining the preceding constructions gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packing}
\begin{split}
 L_*&=\max(L,L_{\mathrm c}),\\
 N&=\max\left\{g(k),L_*,
 \left\lceil\frac{c_k+2L_*}{3}\right\rceil,
 W(p,q),W(p,0),W(0,q)\right\},\\
 \tp(G)&\geq N.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Each term is the size of an available packing or a lower bound on such
a size; the maximum does not require the different constructions to
be compatible with one another.

\section{Cover bounds}\label{sec:covers}

\begin{lemma}[An explicit cover from any core cut]\label{lem:explicit-cut}
Let $C$ be the $k$-vertex core, let $S,T\subseteq C$ be the two
neighborhoods, and let their multiplicities be $p,q\geq0$. For every
$A\subseteq C$, with $j=|A|$, there is a triangle edge cover $F$ with
\begin{align*}
 |F|={}&\binom j2+\binom{k-j}2\\
 &+p\min\{|S\cap A|,|S\setminus A|\}
  +q\min\{|T\cap A|,|T\setminus A|\}.
\end{align*}
Consequently the displayed expression is an upper bound on $\tc(G)$.
No optimality of this cut or of this particular cover is asserted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Give the core parts $A$ and $C\setminus A$ different colors. Put each
outside type on the side containing fewer of its neighbors. Delete
all edges whose endpoints have the same color. Every triangle has a
same-color pair, so the deleted edges meet every triangle.

The deleted core edges are the pairs within $A$ and the pairs within
$C\setminus A$, counted by the two binomial terms. For an outside
vertex of type $S$, the deleted spokes are exactly its neighbors on
its assigned side, giving the smaller of the two intersection counts;
the argument for type $T$ is identical. Distinct outer vertices give
distinct spokes, and no spoke is a core edge. Thus the displayed sum
is the exact number of deleted edges.
\end{proof}



\subsection{Balanced cuts}
Define
\[
 f_k(d)=\max\{0,d/2-k/8,d-k/2\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[A separable cover bound for two arbitrary sizes]
\label{lem:separable-cover}
For two neighborhoods $S,T\subseteq C$ of any sizes $s,t$, with
multiplicities $p,q\geq0$,
\[
 \boxed{\tc(G)\leq c_k+p f_k(s)+q f_k(t).}
\]
No restriction on their intersection or on the unused core is needed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first allow a core vertex to be split fractionally between two parts,
each of total size $k/2$. Put
\[
 b_s=f_k(s),\quad b_t=f_k(t),\qquad
 r_s=s-b_s,\quad r_t=t-b_t,\quad u=|S\cap T|.
\]
The definition implies
\[
 0\leq b_s\leq s/2,\quad r_s\leq k/2,
 \qquad 0\leq b_t\leq t/2,\quad r_t\leq k/2,
 \qquad 2(b_s+b_t)\geq s+t-k/2.
\]
We construct a fractional cut and fix which part contains each outside
type, so that the expected spoke deletion costs per center are at most
$b_s,b_t$, respectively.

If $u\leq b_s+b_t$, retain $r_s$ units of $S$ on one side and $r_t$
units of $T$ on the opposite side. Disjoint choices of these retained
portions exist: first use exclusive vertices, then the common region.
The required common amounts are $(u-b_s)_+$ and $(u-b_t)_+$, whose sum
is at most $u$. Each retained portion has size at most $k/2$, so fill
the remaining spaces to obtain two equal parts. Put each outside type
opposite its designated retained portion. Its deleted spoke mass is
at most $b_s$ or $b_t$.

If $u\geq s+t-k/2-(b_s+b_t)$, place retained portions of sizes
$r_s,r_t$ on the same side. They can be chosen with union size
\[
 \max(r_s,r_t,r_s+r_t-u)\leq k/2,
\]
by overlapping them as much as their common region permits. Fill this
side to size $k/2$ and put both outside types on the opposite side.
Again the deleted spoke masses satisfy the desired bounds.
The two cases cover all values of $u$ because
$2(b_s+b_t)\geq s+t-k/2$.

To round the fractional cut, let $x_v\in[0,1]$ be the fraction of core
vertex $v$ assigned to the first side, so $\sum_v x_v=k/2$.
Keep the outside-type orientations fixed. The spoke cost is an affine
function $d+\sum_v w_v x_v$; the weights $w_v$ may be negative.
Let $A$ contain the $\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ smallest weights, and let
$b\notin A$ have the smallest remaining weight $a=w_b$.
For $k=0$ there is nothing to round. For $k>0$, the ordering gives
\[
 \sum_v w_v x_v-\sum_{v\in A}w_v
 \geq a\left(\sum_v x_v-|A|\right).
\]
Indeed, for $v\in A$ we have
$w_v(x_v-1)\geq a(x_v-1)$, whereas for $v\notin A$ we have
$w_vx_v\geq ax_v$.

For even $k$, the right-hand side is zero, so choose $A$.
For odd $k$, it is $a/2$: choose $A$ if $a\geq0$, and choose
$A\cup\{b\}$ if $a<0$. In the latter case the added cost is
$a\leq a/2$. In each case the chosen integral part has size
$\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ or $\lceil k/2\rceil$, and its spoke cost is
at most $p b_s+q b_t$.

Delete all within-part edges using the explicit cut cover of
Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-cut}. Every such balanced cut has core cost
\[
 \binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{k-|A|}{2}
 =\binom k2-|A|(k-|A|)=c_k.
\]
Thus an actual triangle cover has size at most $c_k+p b_s+q b_t$,
as required.
\end{proof}



\subsection{Deleting the neighborhood shadow}
The \emph{neighborhood shadow} is $E(K_S)\cup E(K_T)$.
Deleting it meets every triangle centered outside the core. We may
then delete further core edges until the residual core is bipartite.

\begin{lemma}[An explicit cut outside the neighborhood shadow]
\label{lem:unequal-shadow}
Suppose $s\leq t$ and put $u=|S\cap T|$. For arbitrary multiplicities,
\[
 \tc(G)\leq
 \begin{cases}
 c_k+(s-u)u,&t\leq k/2,\\
 \binom k2-t(k-t)+su-u^2,&t\geq k/2.
 \end{cases}
\]
The two expressions agree when $t=k/2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $A=S\setminus T$, $B=T\setminus S$, $U=S\cap T$ and
$O=C\setminus(S\cup T)$, with sizes $a=s-u$, $b=t-u$, $u$, and
$o=k-s-t+u$. Delete the entire neighborhood shadow
$E(K_S)\cup E(K_T)$. This destroys all outside-centered triangles.
In the residual core, keep a cut with $A$ on one side, $B\cup U$
on the other, and $z$ vertices of $O$ on the $A$ side. Its edge count is
\[
 ab+a(o-z)+tz+z(o-z)
 =a(k-s)+(k-2a)z-z^2.
\]
Since $a\leq b$, the real maximizer $z_*=k/2-a$ is nonnegative.
Also $a+o=k-t$, so $z_*\leq o$ exactly when $t\leq k/2$.
In that case choose an integer $z$ nearest to $z_*$; the retained
edge count is $\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor-au$. If $t\geq k/2$, choose
$z=o$ instead, retaining $ab+to=t(k-t)-su+u^2$ edges.
The surviving graph has a bipartite core and no outside-centered
triangle. The deleted core edges therefore form a triangle cover.
Subtract the retained edge count from $\binom k2$.
These explicit cuts are not asserted to be maximum cuts.
\end{proof}



We also use this construction for other core partitions in the finite
certificate. If a core part contains $a',b',u',o'$ vertices from
$A,B,U,O$, set $j=a'+b'+u'+o'$, $\ell_S=a'+u'$ and $\ell_T=b'+u'$.
The number of retained edges across the cut and outside the shadow is
\[
 j(k-j)-\ell_S(s-\ell_S)-\ell_T(t-\ell_T)+u'(u-u').
\]
The last term corrects double subtraction of edges in $K_U$.
Consequently deleting all other core edges gives the cover bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-shadow}
 \binom{k}{2}-j(k-j)+\ell_S(s-\ell_S)
       +\ell_T(t-\ell_T)-u'(u-u').
\end{equation}

\subsection{A cut in the common neighborhood}

\begin{lemma}[An unbalanced cut inside the common neighborhood]
\label{lem:common-cut}
Put $m=p+q$, $u=|S\cap T|$, and $E=\binom k2$. For every
$0\leq\ell\leq u$,
\[
 \tc(G)\leq E+ps+qt-\ell(k+m)+\ell^2+\tfrac14.
\]
In particular choose $\ell=\min(u,(k+m)/2)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $B=k+m$, $Q(x)=x^2-Bx$, and choose
$j=\min(u,\lfloor B/2\rfloor)$. Make one core part a $j$-element
subset of $S\cap T$ and place all outside centers on the other side.
The explicit cut construction gives a triangle cover $F$ with
\[
 |F|=\binom j2+\binom{k-j}2+p(s-j)+q(t-j)
     =E+ps+qt+Q(j).
\]
If $u\leq\lfloor B/2\rfloor$, then $j=u$ and, for every $\ell\leq u$,
\[
 Q(\ell)-Q(j)=(u-\ell)(B-u-\ell)\geq0.
\]
Otherwise $j=\lfloor B/2\rfloor$. Since $B$ is an integer,
$0\leq B-2j\leq1$, and completing the square gives
\[
 Q(j)=-\frac{B^2}{4}+\frac{(B-2j)^2}{4}
 \leq-\frac{B^2}{4}+\frac14
 \leq Q(\ell)+\frac14.
\]
Thus the same cover $F$ satisfies the asserted bound for all
$0\leq\ell\leq u$. The minimum of $Q$ on this interval occurs at
$\ell=\min(u,B/2)$, yielding in particular
\[
 \tc(G)\leq E+ps+qt-\frac{B^2}{4}
           +\max(0,B/2-u)^2+\frac14.
\]
\end{proof}



\section{The finite comparison}\label{sec:finite}
The preceding lemmas give a finite arithmetic test once $k,s,t,u,p,q$
are fixed. Compute the integer lower bound $N$ from~\eqref{eq:packing}.
A certificate for these parameters chooses one of the following covers:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the integer bound $\lfloor c_k+p f_k(s)+q f_k(t)\rfloor$;
\item the shadow cover~\eqref{eq:general-shadow} for a recorded core part;
\item the cut cover in Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-cut} for a recorded core part.
\end{enumerate}
For the last two choices, the record gives four nonnegative integers
$a',b',u',o'$ bounded by $s-u,t-u,u,k-s-t+u$. These inequalities ensure
that the part exists in every graph with the specified parameters.
The test checks these bounds and that the cover cost is at most $2N$.
Every accepted record therefore proves $\tc(G)\leq2\tp(G)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cyclic-finite}
Every split graph with at most two active neighborhood types and
$2\leq k\leq42$ satisfies $\tc(G)\leq2\tp(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Computer-assisted proof]
The certificate checks all the integer tuples
\[
\begin{gathered}
2\leq k\leq42,\quad 2\leq s\leq t\leq k,\quad
\max(0,s+t-k)\leq u\leq s,\\
1\leq p\leq h(s),\qquad1\leq q\leq h(t),
\end{gathered}
\]
together with the one-type cases $2\leq s\leq k$, $0\leq p\leq h(s)$,
represented by $t=u=s$ and $q=0$. The numbers of checked tuples are
\[
\begin{array}{lr}
\toprule
\text{one type or clique}&13\,622\\
\text{nested neighborhoods, including identical}&3\,591\,105\\
\text{equal-size crossing neighborhoods}&1\,920\,614\\
\text{unequal-size crossing neighborhoods}&18\,323\,030\\
\midrule
\text{total}&23\,848\,371\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
These counts refer to parameter representations, not isomorphism classes.
For each tuple the checker first tries the separable cover, then eight
shadow cuts: each of $A,B,U$ is placed wholly on either side, and the
chosen side is filled with as many vertices of $O$ as possible without
exceeding $\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ when it is not already larger.
There are 60 tuples for which these candidates do not suffice; the
certificate supplies an explicit cut for each of them. Its validity
is checked by the same part-size and cost inequalities.

The resulting range checks are proved in 2326 Lean modules, including
their assembly into the quantified statement
\texttt{TuzaFiniteData.all\_checked}. The checker soundness theorem
connects acceptance of each record to an actual cover and packing of
every graph it represents. The finite sources, range boundaries,
and 60 cut records are included in the certificate release~\cite{artifact}.
Compression and the parameterization above exhaust the stated graph
class, so the checked comparisons prove the lemma.
\end{proof}

\section{Analytic reductions for large cliques}\label{sec:analytic}
We now assume $k\geq43$ and compressed multiplicities. The first step
handles a single type, small neighborhoods, and small total multiplicity.

\begin{lemma}[The one-type comparison]
\label{lem:single-type-certificate}
Let $k\geq43$, $2\leq d\leq k$, and $0\leq r\leq h(d)$.
Let $H$ consist of a $k$-vertex core and $r$ outside centers with one
common neighborhood of size $d$. Then
\[
 2\tp(H)\geq c_k+r f_k(d).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The $r$ local colors give $r\lfloor d/2\rfloor$ outside triangles,
including at the endpoint $r=h(d)$. The clique, outside, completion,
and sum-coloring bounds therefore give
\[
 2\tp(H)\geq\max\left\{2g(k),\ r(d-1),\
 \frac{2c_k+2r(d-1)}3,\
 \frac{k(k-1)(k-2)/3+r d(d-1)}{k+r}\right\}.
\]
For $d\leq k/4$, the clique bound suffices, since
$2g(k)-c_k\geq(k^2-6k+5)/12>0$.
For $k/4\leq d\leq3k/4$ and $r\geq(k+5)/3$, take one quarter of
the clique bound and three quarters of the completion bound. Subtracting
$c_k+r(d/2-k/8)$ gives at least
\[
 \frac{g(k)-c_k}{2}+\frac{r(k-4)}8
 \geq\frac{k-19}{24}>0.
\]
For $d\geq3k/4$ and $r\geq(k+2)/2$, the outside bound gives
\[
 r(d-1)-[c_k+r(d-k/2)]
 =r(k/2-1)-c_k\geq(2k-5)/4>0.
\]
It remains to justify the sum-coloring comparison in the complementary
multiplicity ranges. For a nonnegative real $m$, define
\[
 F_d(m)=\frac{k(k-1)(k-2)}3+md(d-1)
 -(k+m)\left(\frac{(k-1)^2}{4}+m f_k(d)\right).
\]
The following identities hold in their indicated degree ranges:
\begin{align*}
 48F_d(m)&=P(k,m)+3m(4d-k-m-2)^2,
       &&k/4\leq d\leq3k/4,\\
 12F_d(m)&=Q(k,m)+3m(2d-k-m-1)^2,
       &&3k/4\leq d\leq k,\\
 P(k,m)&=4k^3-24k^2+20k-9mk^2+12mk-12m^2-24m-3m^3,\\
 Q(k,m)&=k^3-6k^2+5k-3m^3-6m^2-6m.
\end{align*}
Both $P,Q$ decrease with $m\geq0$. At their required endpoints,
\begin{align*}
 9P(k,(k+5)/3)&=8h^3+690h^2+15057h+6912>0,
       &&h=k-43\geq0,\\
 8Q(k,(k+2)/2)&=5j^3+177j^2+1615j+747>0,
       &&j=k-17\geq0.
\end{align*}
Thus $F_d(m)\geq0$ when $k/4\leq d\leq3k/4$ and
$m\leq(k+5)/3$, or when $d\geq3k/4$ and $m\leq(k+2)/2$.
Taking $m=r$ proves the remaining cases, since
$c_k\leq(k-1)^2/4$. The comparison includes $r=0$ and $r=h(d)$.
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Small neighborhoods or small total multiplicity]\label{thm:direct-regimes}
Let $G$ have at most two active neighborhood types and a core of size
$k\geq43$. Compress their multiplicities to
\[
 p_0=\min(p,h(s)),\quad q_0=\min(q,h(t)),\quad m=p_0+q_0.
\]
Then Tuza's inequality holds if either
\[
 \boxed{\min(s,t)\leq k/4}
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 \boxed{3m\leq k+5.}
\]
Both statements allow arbitrary overlap; the first places no further
restriction on either multiplicity or on the larger neighborhood size.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compression preserves both parameters, so work with $p_0,q_0$.
Relabel so that $s\leq t$.

If $s\leq k/4$, then $f_k(s)=0$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:separable-cover} gives
$\tc(G)\leq c_k+q_0 f_k(t)$, independently of $p_0$.
The subgraph on the core and the $q_0$ centers of type $T$ is the graph
of Lemma~\ref{lem:single-type-certificate}. Its packing is also a
packing of $G$, so $2\tp(G)\geq c_k+q_0 f_k(t)\geq\tc(G)$.

Now suppose $3m\leq k+5$. The previous case permits us to assume
$s,t>k/4$; $m=0$ is just a clique. Put
\[
 F_d=\frac{k(k-1)(k-2)}3+m d(d-1)
 -(k+m)\left(\frac{(k-1)^2}{4}+m f_k(d)\right).
\]
For $k/4<d\leq3k/4$, the completed-square identity in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:single-type-certificate} gives $48F_d=P(k,m)+3m(4d-k-m-2)^2\geq0$, since
$m\leq(k+5)/3$ and $k\geq43$.
For $d\geq3k/4$, the corresponding identity is
$12F_d=Q(k,m)+3m(2d-k-m-1)^2\geq0$.
Here we use $m\leq(k+5)/3\leq(k+2)/2$ and the positivity bound
for $Q$ proved in the same lemma (already valid for $k\geq17$).

Average $F_s,F_t$ with weights $p_0/m,q_0/m$. Their weighted sum is
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{k(k-1)(k-2)}3+p_0s(s-1)+q_0t(t-1)\\
 &\hspace{8mm}-(k+m)\left(\frac{(k-1)^2}{4}
                  +p_0 f_k(s)+q_0 f_k(t)\right)\geq0.
\end{align*}
The first line divided by $k+m$ is a lower bound for $2\tp(G)$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:sum-coloring}. The last parenthesis is an upper bound for
$\tc(G)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:separable-cover} and
$c_k\leq(k-1)^2/4$. This proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}



\begin{lemma}\label{lem:half-large}
If $s\leq t\leq k/2$ and $k\geq43$, then $\tc(G)\leq2\tp(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:unequal-shadow} gives
$\tc(G)\leq c_k+(s-u)u\leq c_k+k^2/16$. On the other hand,
\[
 2g(k)-c_k-k^2/16\geq\frac{k^2-24k+20}{48}>0
 \qquad(k\geq43).
\]
A packing within the core therefore suffices.
\end{proof}

\section{A continuous comparison for all remaining sizes}\label{sec:interval}
The remaining graphs have two active types. Normalize their parameters by
\[
 x=s/k,\quad y=t/k,\quad w=u/k,\quad
 \alpha=p/k,\quad\beta=q/k,\quad\rho=1/k,\quad\mu=\alpha+\beta.
\]
The previous section leaves only points in the domain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domain}
\begin{gathered}
1/4\leq x\leq y\leq1,\qquad y\geq1/2,\qquad
\max(0,x+y-1)\leq w\leq x,\\
0\leq\rho\leq1/43,\qquad
\rho\leq\alpha\leq x,\qquad\rho\leq\beta\leq y,\\
3(\alpha+\beta)\geq1+5\rho.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Including boundary points and $\rho=0$ only enlarges the domain. In
particular, proving a statement throughout this domain covers arbitrarily
large integer $k$.

\subsection{The bounds to compare}
Put $c^+=(1-\rho)^2/4$ and $f(d)=\max(0,d/2-1/8,d-1/2)$.
We express cover upper bounds divided by $k^2$ and packing lower bounds
on $2\tp(G)/k^2$. The separable and shadow covers give
\[
 D_0=c^++\alpha f(x)+\beta f(y),\qquad
 D_1=(1-\rho)/2-y(1-y)+w(x-w).
\]
Four more candidates are balanced cuts. Fill a part of normalized size
$1/2$ in each of the orders
\[
 (U,A,B,O),\quad(U,B,A,O),\quad(A,U,O,B),\quad(A,O,U,B).
\]
The normalized sizes of these regions are $w,x-w,y-w,1-x-y+w$.
At each step take as much of the next region as the remaining space
permits. For the first two orders place both outside types opposite
the filled part; for the last two place type $S$ opposite it and type
$T$ on its side. The rounding argument in
Lemma~\ref{lem:separable-cover} gives an integral balanced cut of no
greater spoke cost. Its normalized cost is bounded by $D_i=c^++d_i$,
where
\begin{align*}
 d_2&=\alpha(x-1/2)_+
 +\beta\bigl(y-\min(w,1/2)-(1/2-x)_+\bigr)_+,\\
 d_3&=\beta(y-1/2)+\alpha\bigl(x-\min(w,1/2)\bigr),\\
 d_4&=\alpha(x-1/2)_+
 +\beta\max\bigl(y-1/2,w-(x-1/2)_+\bigr),\\
 d_5&=\beta(y-1/2)+\alpha(w-y+1/2)_+.
\end{align*}
These formulas are obtained by counting neighbors placed on each side.
For example, in the first order the filled part takes $\min(w,1/2)$
from $U$, then fills the rest of $S$ up to a total of $\min(x,1/2)$;
any available space $(1/2-x)_+$ is filled from $B$ next. The other
orders are counted in the same way. The feasibility inequalities give
$x-w\leq1/2$ and $1-y\leq1/2$, which justify taking all of $A$ in
the third order and all of $A\cup O$ in the fourth.

Lemma~\ref{lem:common-cut} gives one further bound,
\[
 D_6=c^++\alpha(x-1/2)+\beta(y-1/2)-\mu^2/4
              +\bigl((1+\mu)/2-w\bigr)_+^2.
\]
Set $D=\min_{0\leq i\leq6}D_i$. Then $\tc(G)/k^2\leq D$.

For the packing bounds, define
\begin{align*}
 I&=\alpha(x-\rho)+\beta(y-\rho)
          -\frac{\alpha\beta w(w-\rho)}{xy},\\
 J&=\frac{\alpha x(x-\rho)+\beta y(y-\rho)
          -(\mu-y)_+w(w-\rho)}{y},\qquad X=\max(I,J),\\
 A(r,z)&=\frac{(1-\rho)(1-2\rho)/3
          +r x(x-\rho)+z y(y-\rho)}{1+r+z},\\
 P&=\max\left\{\frac{(1-\rho)(1-2\rho)}3,\ X,
       \frac{1-2\rho}{6}+\frac{2X}{3},
       A(\alpha,\beta),A(\alpha,0),A(0,\beta)\right\}.
\end{align*}
These satisfy $2\tp(G)/k^2\geq P$. Here $I$ and $J$ are relaxed
versions of the independent and coupled allocation bounds. The
independent expression $r_Se_S+r_Te_T-r_Sr_Te_U$ increases with each
rate on $[0,1]^2$, so replacing $p/h(s),q/h(t)$ by $p/s,q/t$ is valid.
For the coupled expression, with $s\leq t$, increasing the palette
size from $h(t)$ to $t$ decreases
\[
 \frac{p\binom{s}{2}+q\binom{t}{2}-(p+q-H)_+\binom{u}{2}}{H}.
\]
Indeed, below $H=p+q$ this equals
$\binom{u}{2}+[p(\binom{s}{2}-\binom{u}{2})+
q(\binom{t}{2}-\binom{u}{2})]/H$, and above it the numerator is
constant. The remaining terms follow from greedy completion and
sum-coloring, discarding integer rounding.

\subsection{An auxiliary comparison}

\begin{corollary}[A common-neighborhood criterion]
\label{cor:common-cut}
Suppose $k\geq34$ and $p+q=m\leq2k$. Then Tuza's inequality holds if
\[
 \boxed{4(k+m)(k+m-2u)_+^2\leq k(k^2+3m^2).}
\]
After multiplicity compression, the condition $m\leq2k$ is automatic.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use $x=s/k$, $y=t/k$, $\alpha=p/k$, $\beta=q/k$, $\mu=m/k$,
$\rho=1/k$, and put $e=((1+\mu)/2-u/k)_+$.
The preceding cover bound, divided by $k^2$, is
\[
 C=\frac{(1-\rho)^2}{4}+\alpha x+\beta y
                      -\frac\mu2-\frac{\mu^2}{4}+e^2.
\]
Let $A$ be the normalized sum-coloring lower bound on $2\tp(G)$.
Completing squares gives the identity
\begin{align*}
 (1+\mu)(A-C)
 &=B(\mu,\rho)-(1+\mu)e^2\\
 &\quad+\alpha\left(x-\frac{1+\mu+\rho}{2}\right)^2
       +\beta\left(y-\frac{1+\mu+\rho}{2}\right)^2,\\
 B(\mu,\rho)
 &=\frac1{12}+\frac{\mu^2}{4}
       -\frac{(1+\mu^2)\rho}{2}
       +\left(\frac5{12}-\frac\mu2\right)\rho^2.
\end{align*}
For $0\leq\mu\leq2$ and $0\leq\rho\leq1/34$,
\[
 B(\mu,\rho)\geq
 \frac1{12}-\frac1{68}-\frac7{12\cdot34^2}
             +\left(\frac14-\frac1{68}\right)\mu^2
 =\frac{315}{4624}+\frac4{17}\mu^2
 \geq\frac{1+3\mu^2}{16}.
\]
The stated criterion is exactly
$16(1+\mu)e^2\leq1+3\mu^2$. Thus $A\geq C$.
\end{proof}



\subsection{The interval certificate}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cyclic-interval}
On the domain~\eqref{eq:domain}, $P\geq D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Computer-assisted proof]
The certificate partitions a box enclosing~\eqref{eq:domain} into
finitely many boxes with rational endpoints. A box may first be
contracted using the linear inequalities in~\eqref{eq:domain}; each
contraction preserves every feasible point. An empty box needs no
further check. A nonempty terminal box is accepted by one of two tests.

The first test verifies
\[
 (1+\mu)\bigl((1+\mu)/2-w\bigr)_+^2
 \leq (1+3\mu^2)/16
\]
throughout the box. The completed-square calculation in
Corollary~\ref{cor:common-cut} gives $A(\alpha,\beta)\geq D_6$ there:
the domain has $\rho\leq1/43<1/34$ and $\mu\leq2$.
The second test directly compares the other bounds. Subtract $c^+$
from every packing term and every cover term, preserving their
difference. Evaluate each expression by rational interval arithmetic.
Accept if the lower endpoint of some packing interval is at least
the upper endpoint of some cover interval. The auxiliary identity
\[
 0\leq w(x-w)\leq x^2/4
\]
can sharpen the shadow-cover interval. For reference, the clique gap is
\[
 \frac{(1-\rho)(1-2\rho)}3-c^+
 =\frac1{12}-\frac\rho2+\frac{5\rho^2}{12}.
\]

Addition, multiplication, squaring, minimum, maximum, and division by
a positive interval use bounds proved to enclose every possible value.
All arithmetic is rational; no floating-point estimate is a proof step.
A nonterminal node records a coordinate split and both children.
The split-coverage proof combines the two child conclusions.
Four adjacent initial slabs cover the domain:
\[
\begin{array}{lr}
\toprule
x\text{ interval}&\text{accepted leaves}\\
\midrule
{[1/4,7/16]}&5\,604\\
{[7/16,5/8]}&116\,226\\
{[5/8,13/16]}&55\,861\\
{[13/16,1]}&815\\
\midrule
\text{total}&178\,506\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
The complete trees, including all $357\,008$ nodes, are supplied in
the certificate sources~\cite{artifact}. Lean checks the terminal
comparisons, the soundness of contractions and interval evaluation,
and the coverage of every split and root. The resulting theorem
\texttt{TuzaIntervalData.all\_checked} proves the comparison on the
whole domain. Every leaf is accepted; no sampled-point inference is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-types-all}]
Delete inactive vertices and compress multiplicities. Both operations
preserve $\tc$ and $\tp$. For $k\leq1$ the graph has no triangles;
for $2\leq k\leq42$ apply Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclic-finite}.

Suppose $k\geq43$. The one-type comparison handles zero or one active
type. With two types, order $s\leq t$. Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-regimes}
handles $s\leq k/4$ and $3(p+q)\leq k+5$;
Lemma~\ref{lem:half-large} handles $t\leq k/2$.
Every remaining instance normalizes to a point
in~\eqref{eq:domain}. Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclic-interval} and the graph
bounds then give
\[
 \frac{2\tp(G)}{k^2}\geq P\geq D\geq\frac{\tc(G)}{k^2}.
\]
These cases exhaust the graph class in the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Formal verification and reproducibility}\label{sec:formal}
The public theorem is
\texttt{TuzaTwoTypes.tuza\_of\_two\_active\_types} in
\path{formal/Result.lean}. Its hypotheses state that $G$ is a finite
simple graph, $C$ is a clique, its complement is independent, and the
active outside neighborhoods form a set of cardinality at most two.
The conclusion uses the actual optimum cover and packing numbers.

The formal proof is divided into four main modules:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lp{83mm}}
\toprule
\texttt{GraphReductions}&Graph constructions, multiplicity compression,
integer bounds and finite-checker soundness.\\
\texttt{CertificateKernel}&Rational intervals, domain contractions,
expression evaluation and tree-checker soundness.\\
\texttt{NormalizedBounds}&Analytic reductions and the connection between
normalized inequalities and graph optima.\\
\texttt{Result}&The final theorem, combining both certificate families.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The finite certificate adds 2326 modules. The continuous certificate
adds 860: 347 subtree modules, four roots, 508 helper modules, and one
domain-coverage module. Together with the four main modules these give
3190 project modules.

The public source build completed on 8 October 2026 with Lean 4.34.0
and Mathlib revision \texttt{5ed2965256430c3649e86755f9576b54eca72435}.
It compiled every project module from source, with no precompiled
project modules as inputs. The run, source hashes, compiler output,
and verification report are linked from the repository~\cite{artifact}.
The certificate release contains the complete generated Lean sources;
the README gives the reproduction commands.

Certificate generators supply proof terms, not axioms. Lean checks
the arithmetic proofs with \texttt{decide +kernel} and checks the
soundness theorems connecting them to the graph statement. The final
theorem depends only on \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and
\texttt{Quot.sound}. The project sources contain no admitted proof,
custom axiom, unsafe declaration, or native decision shortcut.
A deliberately false arithmetic proof was rejected in the same build.
For Mantel's bound the formalization uses Mathlib's Tur\'an theorem;
Lemma~\ref{lem:completion} also gives its elementary degree-sum proof.
